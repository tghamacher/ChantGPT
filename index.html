      How it works

      1. Open a PDF, page images, or a manuscript online. Your files never leave your browser.
      2. Find staves on the page, or drag a box around a staff, from its first note to its last, to add it yourself.
      3. Read page: the recognizer reads the notes. Doubtful neumes are orange; the rest are red until you accept them, then green.
      4. Check and correct, then Accept each staff. Save your work as a document to carry on later.

      Right-click a neume, a syllable, a staff or the page for what you can do with it. Or paste a chant's GABC (Chant, on the right) and place it on a staff: it's drawn over the page so you can see where they differ.

      The mouse

      • Drag a staff to move it; drag a line's dot to move that end; drag the bar at either end to lengthen, shorten or tilt it. Double-click a staff to bend it there; double-click a bend's bar to straighten it.
      • Drag a neume up or down by steps (its GABC changes with it), or along the staff. Shift-drag moves one note.
      • Right-click a note in a neume to make it a punctum, virga, inclinatum, quilisma or oriscus, or to split the neume before it. J joins a neume to the next.
      • Read text reads the words under the notes and puts each syllable under its neume (the selected staff, or the whole page). For printed books so far.
      • ▶ in the toolbar (or Space) plays the page from what's selected, marking each neume as it sounds: a flat holds to the word's end or the next bar, as chant has it. Listen for a wrong note or a missing flat.
      • Right-click a neume for the text sung to it (T), or to make it part of the syllable before (a melisma).
      • Select a neume or a clef to type its GABC in the panel (fgh, f3); right-click a clef to make it another (C1–C4, F1–F4, with a flat).
      • Click the words under a neume to correct them, or drag them into place.
